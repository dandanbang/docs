Writing is not always the easiest way in. Some days the thought arrives faster than you can type it, some days a photograph says it better, and some days it is already written — on paper.

</Frame>

## Speak it

Tap **Voice** in the composer and talk. Life Note transcribes what you said and drops the text straight into your entry, where you can edit it like anything else.

It is the fastest way to journal when you are walking, driving, or simply too tired to type — and it tends to be more honest, because you don't get to edit yourself mid-sentence.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Press to speak">
    Tap the **Voice** button in the journal composer and allow microphone access the first time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Say what's on your mind">
    Pause and resume as much as you like. Recordings can run up to five minutes each.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Stop, and read it back">
    Your transcript appears in the composer. Edit it, add to it, or record another pass.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Your audio is sent for transcription and then deleted. Only the transcript is kept, and it is yours alone.
</Note>

## Photograph it

Tap **Add Photo** to attach an image to an entry — the view from a trip, a person you wrote about, a page you want to remember. Photos live inside the entry itself and appear in your timeline alongside the words.

<Info>
  Paid subscribers get full-resolution photos. On the free plan, images are stored at a smaller size.
</Info>

## Scan a paper page

If you keep a paper journal, you don't have to retype it. In the mobile app, use **Scan Journal** to photograph a page with your camera or pick a photo from your library. Life Note extracts the handwriting into text you can edit and save as a normal entry.

<Tip>
  Flat page, good light, no shadow across the writing. That is most of the battle with handwriting recognition.
</Tip>
